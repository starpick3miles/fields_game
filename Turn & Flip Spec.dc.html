Turn & Flip: teacher demo on iPad

A 3D block puzzle that teaches rotation and reflection to K–2 students. In v2 the teacher operates it on an iPad held in landscape and mirrored to a classroom screen. The game page is built to this spec; rows marked v2 are what changed from v1. Sizes are CSS px at a scale of 1.

4. Behavior

Turn animation380 ms ease-in-out, spinning flat with a 16 px lift
Flip animation600 ms, lifting 110 px and turning over
Solve (match)v2 When the piece matches the hole, the hole's edge pulses in accent and the piece waits 2 s. If the teacher drags the piece onto the hole in that time, it drops in under the finger. If not, it arcs into the hole by itself over 800 ms as in v1. Touching the piece stops the countdown; letting go away from the hole restarts it. Either way the result dialog opens once the piece has landed, and neither way adds a move.
Color meaningCyan side up means turned only. Magenta side up means flipped an odd number of times.
Move count+1 per turn, flip, tap, placement or answer. Undo restores the count. v2 A gesture counts exactly like its button. A drag that springs back counts nothing.
Stars3 stars at the minimum move count (+2 allowed in level 5), 2 stars within 4 extra moves, otherwise 1. Using a hint caps the score at 2. The best score is saved. v2.1 It is kept per puzzle, and a level tab shows the lowest of its puzzles, so three stars on the tab means three stars on every puzzle.
HintLevels 1, 2 and 5: rings the next move. Level 3: removes one wrong choice. Level 4: marks one cell to fix. v2 Level 5 accepts any valid tiling: the hint is worked out from the pieces already on the board, and rings Undo only when the board can no longer be completed. (v1 knew one solution and rejected the others.)
SoundSynthesized tones for turn, flip, place, wrong and solve. There is a toggle in the header.
SavingCurrent level, current puzzle and best stars are stored in the browser (localStorage key turnflip.v1). v2 The Score and Sound switches are saved there too.
Math languagev2 Formal terms everywhere, during play and in the result dialog. (v1 used none during play.)

5. Touch and gestures v2

Every gesture has a button that does the same thing. Gestures are worked out from one finger wherever possible, because the other hand is holding the iPad.

GestureWhereResult
Turn: drag the rimOn the bench disc, outside the piece (levels 1, 2, 5)The piece follows the finger around the disc. On release it settles to the nearest quarter turn. Each quarter turn passed is one move, logged as ↻ or ↺. Released under 45° from the start, it settles back and counts nothing.
Turn: two-finger twistOn the pieceSame as dragging the rim.
Flip: flickOn the piece (levels 2, 5)A quick stroke across the piece: at least 40 px in under 250 ms. Sideways flicks ↔, up or down flicks ↕, a slanted flick flips ⤡ (level 2 only). The stroke direction is read in 45° sectors.
Place: dragFrom the piece onto the board (level 5)A touch that stays down past 250 ms, or moves slower than a flick, picks the piece up. The ghost follows, snapped to the grid. Release on a valid spot places it. Release anywhere else and it springs back to the bench in 250 ms with the wrong tone.
Try the hole: dragFrom the piece onto the hole (levels 1, 2)The piece can be picked up at any time, with the same pick-up rule as placing. Released over the hole when it matches, it drops in and the level is solved. Released over the hole when it does not match, it rests on top for 400 ms so the class can see the mismatch, then springs back to the bench with the wrong tone. No move is counted.
Place: tap, tapOn a board cell (level 5)The first tap shows the ghost there. A second tap on the ghost places it. Tapping another cell moves the ghost.
PaintRight half of the mirror board (level 4)Tap toggles one cell. Dragging across cells sets each one to the state the first cell changed to. Each cell changed is one move.

Making taps land

Cause in v1The board is tilted 50°, so a 56 px cell is drawn about 67 wide and 40 tall at iPad scale, under the 44 px a finger needs. Taps during a turn or flip animation were thrown away, which reads as a missed tap.
Hit testingThe touch point is projected onto the table plane and snapped to the nearest cell, instead of relying on which element is under the finger. A touch up to half a cell outside the board edge still counts for the edge cell.
Cubes in the wayA touch on any visible face of a standing cube belongs to that cube's cell, not the cell drawn behind it.
Input during animationOne input is held and played when the animation ends. Further inputs replace the held one.
Tap toleranceA touch is a tap if it lifts within 10 px of where it landed. The action fires on lift.
Stray touchesWhile one finger is dragging, other touches on the stage are ignored (except the second finger of a twist). Double-tap zoom and long-press callouts are switched off on the game page.
FeedbackEvery accepted touch gives a tone and a visible change within 100 ms.
